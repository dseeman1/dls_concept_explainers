DLS Simulator

Watch Brownian motion, size a particle

Particles diffusing through the scattering volume make the detected intensity fluctuate. Small particles diffuse quickly and the signal flickers fast; large particles drift slowly and it varies slowly. The autocorrelation function captures that timescale, and Stokes–Einstein turns it into a size.

Configuration: 640 nm laser · 90° scattering · water at 25 °C · correlator delay 1 µs to 300 ms.

Autocorrelation function

The time when decay starts indicates the mean size; the curve shifts right for larger, slower particles.

g2(τ) − 1 = β · exp(−2Γτ)  ,   Γ = Dt · q2
measured g2(τ) − 1 ideal fit τ axis 1 µs → 300 ms (log)
Stokes–Einstein: D = kBT / (3πηd), η = 0.890 mPa·s, n = 1.330 (water, 25 °C). Scattering vector q = (4πn/λ₀)·sin(θ/2). The scattered field is modeled as a sum of detuned sinusoids whose beat timescale scales with the correlation time 1/Γ; intensity is its square. The black curve is the autocorrelation computed live from that simulated signal — so it jitters and firms up as samples accumulate, the way a real correlator does — with the ideal exp(−2Γτ) drawn in maroon for reference.
