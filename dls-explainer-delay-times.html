Correlator Layout and the Range of Measurable Dynamics

The particle diameter defaults to d = 88 nm and can be changed. The variable of interest is the correlator layout: the first (shortest) and last (longest) delay times that bound the autocorrelation function. These bounds set the range of dynamics the instrument can resolve. If the last delay is too short, the decay is clipped before it completes; if the first delay is too long, the early part of the decay is not sampled. Brookhaven’s BI-TurboCorr USB correlator card provides delay times spanning this range, with a first delay as short as a few nanoseconds. Set the bounds below and observe where g2(τ)−1 falls within them.

Default: 88 nm particle · 640 nm laser · 90° scattering · water at 25 °C. Variable: particle diameter, plus first & last delay (100 ns – 10 s), as configured on the BI-TurboCorr.

Diameter accepts nm or µm, e.g. 88 nm, 0.5 um (range 0.5 nm – 10 µm). Delays accept ns/us/ms/s, e.g. 100 ns, 2 ms (range 100 ns – 10 s; last must exceed first).
